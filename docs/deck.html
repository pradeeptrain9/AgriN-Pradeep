The problem

Advice exists. It just isn't about your field.

Advisories are issued per district. A district is thousands of square kilometres and hundreds of soil types. Two farmers three kilometres apart, on sand and on clay, get the same irrigation advice. One of them is wrong.

The advice arrives as a broadcast — a bulletin, an SMS blast — with no way to ask "what about mine?"

And the new AI farm assistants hallucinate doses. A chatbot that invents "apply 120 kg of urea" is not a smaller version of good advice. It is a crop-failure liability with a friendly tone.

The gap we target

Per-field, computed, explainable — and in a language the farmer actually reads or hears.

The governing rule

The model never produces a number

Every recommended quantity is computed deterministically. Gemini only translates and explains those numbers — it never produces them.

This is not a prompt instruction. ai/guard.py extracts every figure the model wrote and every figure the engine computed, and rejects the narration if the model produced one the engine did not.

Rejection triggers one corrective round naming the offending figures. Fail twice and the farmer sees the deterministic template — the same advice, plainer words.

Proof it belongs to the system, not the vendor

The cloud model was swapped from one provider to Gemini in an afternoon. guard.py was not touched. Not one line.

If the safety property had lived in the vendor integration, replacing the vendor would have broken it.

The same principle governs chemicals: pesticide doses and pre-harvest intervals are served only from rows verified against the national register. That table ships empty, so an unverified node gives cultural and preventive advice only. A model can never introduce a product name.

On-device vision

It abstains more often than it answers

0.981
Accuracy of predictions actually shown to a farmer — those clearing the on-device confidence gate.
44.4%
Of photographs answered on the handset. The rest escalate to Gemini or return inconclusive.
1.14 MB
Model size. Runs offline, on a low-end Android, at no data cost.

The gate is the product. Raw model accuracy is 0.786. Confidence, margin and entropy thresholds convert that into 0.981 on what reaches a farmer, by refusing the rest.

A wrong confident answer costs a season. "Unknown" costs a second photo.

What we will not claim

We hold a 0.994 figure on a 1,677-image test slice. It is the same collection as part of the training data and covers 4 of 10 classes. Our own model card says it must not be quoted as a cross-collection result — so it isn't, here or anywhere.

Real data, India first

Live sources — and one that mattered more than expected

LayerSource
SatelliteCopernicus Sentinel-2 L2A, SCL cloud-masked
WeatherOpen-Meteo → NASA POWER → MET Norway
SoilSoil Health Card → farmer feel-test → SoilGrids → ISRO Bhuvan
AgronomyFAO-56 tables 11, 12, 19, 22

Every figure carries its provenance to the farmer's screen — including when it is a regional estimate rather than a measurement of their own field.

SoilGrids returns null across all of India

Verified against a Brazilian control, which answers normally. So the node fell back to assuming loam for every field in the country.

ISRO's Bhuvan national soil map says the test field is loamy sand. FAO-56 Table 19: loam holds 130 mm of available water per metre; loamy sand holds 80.

The node had been overstating that field's water reservoir by more than half — directly into the irrigation decision.

Fallback chains, never blends. A figure is always from one named source, so an error can be traced to it rather than averaged into invisibility.

Depth across India

One node per state. Same code, sovereign data.

Nodes exchange models and aggregate statistics. Never raw farmer data.

  • District-level aggregates only, with k-anonymity and complementary suppression enforced structurally
  • Every published payload Ed25519-signed over canonical JSON, so it cannot be replayed under another node's name
  • A publish is aborted if an identifier is detected in the payload — checked again on the BigQuery export path
  • EXIF and GPS stripped from every uploaded photograph

A state department runs its own node, holds its own farmers' data, and still contributes to — and benefits from — a national picture.

Why this shape, not a central platform

A single national database of every farmer's field boundaries and yields is a target and a governance problem. Federation means no one has to hold it.

It is also the only shape that survives the politics: states adopt what they control.

Standards are boring on purpose — AGROVOC crop concepts, UCUM units, GeoJSON RFC 7946, OpenAPI 3.1 — so a second implementation can interoperate without asking us.

Live, not planned. The running node declares itself IN-MH at /.well-known/agrin-node, and serves the real 1.1 MB classifier with its SHA-256. Edit the state out of a signed aggregate and the signature fails.

Deployability

It is deployed, and it grades itself

A live node runs at agrin-node-in.onrender.com. The same container deploys to Cloud Run unchanged, for a ministry that already lives inside Google Cloud.

GET /ready is the node's own honest self-assessment. Every check names the user-visible consequence, not an internal state, and is graded blocker degraded ok.

It currently reports two blockers: no SMS provider, so no farmer can sign in yet — and the fixed demo code that let you sign in, which it refuses to call acceptable now that the node holds someone else's data. It says both in public, rather than waiting to be found out.

Honest status

What is not finished

A prototype that hides its gaps is harder to deploy, not easier. Assessed against all nine DPG Alliance indicators — including the two it does not meet.

Blocker

SMS delivery. No farmer can sign in until a DLT-registered aggregator is configured. Deliberate: we will not enrol a farmer on a login we cannot deliver.

Partial

Platform independence. The node now calls Google. Every service fails closed and the engine needs none of them — but keyless, the farmer loses plain language in their own tongue. We downgraded our own DPG score rather than argue.

Partial

Independent review. No external agronomic review of the recommendation logic, and no DPIA. Both need people, not code. Neither is bluffed.

Also stated plainly in the repo: the disease model is rice-only, so the other 13 crops route to Gemini and receive a name with no treatment attached; the Earth Engine adapter has not been verified against the live service; and Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial, so a paid deployment needs their commercial tier or a self-hosted instance.

Impact & what happens next

Water and nitrogen, which is where the leverage is

Punjab's groundwater is falling. Nitrogen is over-applied across the Indo-Gangetic plain. Both are decisions taken field by field, weekly, by millions of people working from a district bulletin.

AgriN does not ask a farmer to trust a model. It tells them the number, where it came from, and how confident it is — including when the answer is "we don't know".

  • SDG 2.3 / 2.4 — smallholder productivity and resilience
  • SDG 6.4 — water-use efficiency, via the AWD paddy model
  • SDG 13 — reduced methane from AWD and reduced nitrogen loss
